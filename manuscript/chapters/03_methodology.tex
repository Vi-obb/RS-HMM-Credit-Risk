\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

\section{Data and Study Population}

The study combined Freddie Mac Single-Family Loan-Level Dataset sample files with macroeconomic series from Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) \citep{freddiemac_loanlevel,fred_database}. Freddie Mac origination records supplied initial borrower and contract characteristics. Monthly performance records supplied delinquency status, balance, interest rate, loan age, remaining term, and termination information. The available sample cohorts covered 2015--2025. The twelve-month follow-up rule left eligible observations from the 2015--2024 cohorts and a common endpoint of September 2024.

Origination and performance files were linked by the loan sequence identifier. Calendar dates were normalised to the first day of each month. Delinquency status in the servicing data was converted from months delinquent to days past due by multiplying non-negative numeric values by 30. Loan-level records were ordered by identifier and month before the outcome and payment-history variables were calculated.

The unit of analysis was a loan-month. Let $D_{i,t}$ denote days past due for loan $i$ in month $t$. A loan-month entered the risk set when the loan was active, $D_{i,t}<90$, the servicing record extended through $t+12$, and no terminal servicing event occurred on or before $t+12$. The eligible set was
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}
=
\left\{(i,t):D_{i,t}<90,\ t+12\leq T_i^{\max},\
T_i^{\mathrm{term}}>t+12\ \text{or }T_i^{\mathrm{term}}\text{ is missing}\right\},
\label{eq:risk-set}
\end{equation}
where $T_i^{\max}$ is the last observed month and $T_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ is the first terminal month. This rule excluded observations with incomplete forward follow-up instead of treating them as non-events.

The primary outcome was
\begin{equation}
Y_{i,t}^{(12)}
=
\mathbb{I}\!\left\{\max_{h=1,\ldots,12}D_{i,t+h}\geq90\right\},
\qquad (i,t)\in\mathcal{R}.
\label{eq:primary-label}
\end{equation}
The conditional PD was $\pi_{i,t}^{(12)}=\Pr(Y_{i,t}^{(12)}=1\mid\mathcal{F}_t)$. All predictors were measured at origination or constructed from information observed by month $t$.

\section{Loan-Level Predictors}

The borrower and loan vector $X_{i,t}$ contained 14 origination or current-account variables and 18 payment-history variables. The first group comprised credit score, mortgage-insurance percentage, number of units, original combined loan-to-value ratio, original debt-to-income ratio, original unpaid principal balance, original loan-to-value ratio, original interest rate, original term, number of borrowers, loan age, remaining months to legal maturity, current unpaid principal balance, and current interest rate.

The payment-history group comprised current delinquency in months; current 30- and 60-day delinquency indicators; recent 30- and 60-day delinquency within three months; ever-30- and ever-60-day indicators; months since 30- and 60-day delinquency; counts of 30- and 60-day delinquent months over three, six, and twelve months; a cure-from-30-days indicator; cumulative 30-day cures; and months since a 30-day cure. These features were calculated within loan and used observations through month $t$.

The rolling counts were based on cumulative within-loan event counts and their lags. A recent indicator equalled one when the corresponding three-month count was positive. A cure occurred when a loan moved from at least 30 days past due in month $t-1$ to below 30 days past due in month $t$. For loans with no prior delinquency or cure, the elapsed-month variable was measured from the first observed loan month plus one. This convention provided a finite history measure without using a later event date.

\section{Macroeconomic Variables and Factors}

The macroeconomic data contained CPI, the Federal Funds rate, unemployment, the FHFA house-price index, the 30-year mortgage rate, the 10-year Treasury rate, the 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread, initial unemployment claims, non-farm payrolls, real disposable personal income, the National Financial Conditions Index, and consumer sentiment. Weekly and daily observations were averaged to monthly frequency. The retained transformations were twelve-month inflation, the policy rate, unemployment and its monthly change, twelve-month house-price growth, the mortgage rate, the mortgage--Treasury spread, the long rate and its monthly change, the yield curve, initial claims and their monthly change, twelve-month payroll and real-income growth, financial conditions, consumer sentiment, and its monthly change.

The HMM did not receive the 17 transformed series directly. Each series was oriented so that a larger value represented greater stress and standardised. Available series were then averaged within four groups: housing/collateral, rate/refinancing, labour/income, and financial conditions. For group $g$,
\begin{equation}
F_{t,g}
=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_g|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}_g}
\frac{a_jM_{t,j}-\overline{a_jM_j}}{s(a_jM_j)},
\label{eq:interpretable-factor}
\end{equation}
where $a_j\in\{-1,1\}$ sets the adverse direction. The four factors were standardised again before HMM estimation. Equal weighting retained a direct mapping from the source series to each factor.

The HMM estimation window was January 2014--September 2024. It included a twelve-month margin before the first mortgage observation. The factor transformations, scaler, and HMM parameters were estimated once over this complete window.

Inflation remained in the 17-variable observed macroeconomic vector used by the additive baseline. It was not assigned to one of the four retained HMM factor groups. The HMM source set therefore differed from the complete macro-augmented logistic input. The additive baseline received every available transformed macro series, while the HMM received the four grouped factors recorded in the retained HMM artefact.

\section{Two-State Hidden Markov Model}

Let $R_t\in\{0,1\}$ denote the latent macroeconomic state and let $Z_t$ be the four-dimensional standardised factor vector. The state process followed a first-order Markov chain,
\begin{equation}
\Pr(R_t=k\mid R_{t-1}=j)=p_{jk},
\qquad
P=
\begin{bmatrix}
p_{00}&p_{01}\\
p_{10}&p_{11}
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:hmm-transition}
\end{equation}
and the emissions followed state-specific multivariate Gaussian distributions,
\begin{equation}
Z_t\mid R_t=k\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\Sigma_k),
\qquad k\in\{0,1\}.
\label{eq:gaussian-emission}
\end{equation}
Both covariance matrices were unrestricted. The model used two states, full covariance matrices, a maximum of 500 estimation iterations, and a fixed random seed of 42. Parameters were estimated by the expectation-maximisation procedure for Gaussian HMMs \citep{baum1970maximization,bilmes1998em}.

For a latent state path $R_{1:T}$ and observed factors $Z_{1:T}$, the joint density was
\begin{equation}
f(Z_{1:T},R_{1:T};\Theta)
=
\delta_{R_1}
\prod_{t=2}^{T}p_{R_{t-1},R_t}
\prod_{t=1}^{T}\phi_4(Z_t;\mu_{R_t},\Sigma_{R_t}),
\label{eq:hmm-joint-density}
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ is the initial state distribution and $\phi_4$ is the four-dimensional Gaussian density. The expectation step calculated posterior state and transition probabilities under the current parameter values. The maximisation step updated the initial probabilities, transition matrix, state means, and covariance matrices.

The fitted state labels were oriented after estimation. If $\widehat\mu_{k,g}$ is the fitted mean of adverse-oriented factor $g$ in state $k$, the state score was
\begin{equation}
s_k=\sum_{g=1}^{4}\widehat\mu_{k,g}.
\label{eq:state-stress-score}
\end{equation}
The state with the larger $s_k$ was labelled Stress. This rule used all four HMM inputs and did not define stress from the frequency or chronology of the fitted state.

The state with the larger sum of adverse-oriented fitted factor means was labelled Stress. The forward recursion produced
\begin{align}
\alpha_1(k)&\propto\delta_k f_k(Z_1),\\
\alpha_t(k)&\propto f_k(Z_t)\sum_{j=0}^{1}\alpha_{t-1}(j)p_{jk},
\end{align}
with normalisation at each month. The retained macroeconomic input was
\begin{equation}
q_t
=
\Pr(R_t=\mathrm{Stress}\mid Z_{1:t};\widehat\Theta).
\label{eq:filtered-stress}
\end{equation}
This was a filtered rather than smoothed probability. It did not condition on factor observations after month $t$ in the state recursion. It remained conditional on transformations and HMM parameters estimated over the full January 2014--September 2024 window.

The fitted output also contained $\mathbb{I}(q_t\geq0.5)$, three-, six-, and twelve-month lags of $q_t$, the monthly change $\Delta q_t$, and the duration of the current sequence above 0.5. The primary regime-aware model used only $q_t$. The hard indicator was used for descriptive regime slices. The smaller dynamic diagnostic used $q_t$, $\Delta q_t$, and duration. The lagged values were retained in the HMM output but were not included in the reported dynamic model.

\section{Loan-Level PD Models}

All PD specifications were regularised logistic models. Numerical inputs and interaction terms were standardised within each fitted pipeline. The borrower-only baseline was
\begin{equation}
\pi_{i,t}^{(B)}
=
\sigma\!\left(\beta_0+X_{i,t}^{\top}\beta\right),
\label{eq:borrower-baseline}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(u)=(1+e^{-u})^{-1}$. The macro-augmented baseline added the 17 transformed macroeconomic variables $M_t$:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{i,t}^{(M)}
=
\sigma\!\left(\beta_0+X_{i,t}^{\top}\beta+M_t^{\top}\gamma\right).
\label{eq:macro-baseline}
\end{equation}

The regime-aware model used $q_t$ and selected interactions:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{i,t}^{(R)}
=
\sigma\!\left(\beta_0+X_{i,t}^{\top}\beta+\gamma_Rq_t
+q_tW_{i,t}^{\top}\theta\right),
\label{eq:regime-aware-model}
\end{equation}
where $W_{i,t}$ contained credit score, original combined loan-to-value ratio, original debt-to-income ratio, original loan-to-value ratio, original interest rate, current delinquency in months, recent 60-day delinquency within three months, and months since 30-day delinquency. The marginal log-odds coefficient for an interacted variable $W_{i,t,j}$ was $\beta_j+q_t\theta_j$. The specification therefore allowed selected loan-level sensitivities to vary continuously with the estimated stress probability.

Three additional models separated components of this structure. A stress-level model added $q_t$ to $X_{i,t}$ without interactions. A regime-aware plus raw-macro model combined Equation~\eqref{eq:regime-aware-model} with $M_t$. A dynamic-stress diagnostic added $\Delta q_t=q_t-q_{t-1}$ and the number of consecutive months for which $q_t\geq0.5$. These models were evaluated on the same target and chronological samples.

The implementation used stochastic-gradient logistic regression with an $L_2$ penalty. Candidate penalty values were $\{3\times10^{-6},10^{-5},3\times10^{-5}\}$. Each value was evaluated on deterministic samples of 500,000 training and 500,000 validation observations. The value with the lowest validation Brier score was selected, and the model was then fitted to the complete training sample.

For coefficient vector $\omega$ and intercept $\beta_0$, estimation minimised a penalised Bernoulli objective of the form
\begin{equation}
-\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{train}}}
\left[Y_{i,t}^{(12)}\log\pi_{i,t}+(1-Y_{i,t}^{(12)})\log(1-\pi_{i,t})\right]
+\lambda\lVert\omega\rVert_2^2.
\label{eq:penalised-logit}
\end{equation}
The intercept was separate from the penalised feature vector. Standardisation was fitted within the model pipeline. The coefficient artefact therefore recorded effects on the standardised scale. The empirical analysis used the coefficients to define an explicit predictive model rather than to make causal claims.

\section{Chronological Validation and Calibration}

The 117 model months were ordered before splitting. The first 70\% formed the training period, the next 15\% formed the validation period, and the final 15\% formed the test period. This produced January 2015--September 2021 for training, October 2021--March 2023 for validation, and April 2023--September 2024 for testing. Model selection used the validation period. Test outcomes were reserved for final evaluation.

Raw probabilities were calibrated using the complete validation predictions. The primary method was Platt scaling,
\begin{equation}
\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{Platt}}
=
\sigma\!\left(a+b\,\operatorname{logit}(\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{raw}})\right).
\label{eq:platt-calibration}
\end{equation}
Intercept-only recalibration and isotonic regression were retained as sensitivity analyses. The fitted calibration maps were applied unchanged to training, validation, and test predictions. All primary comparisons used the Platt-calibrated probability.

The intercept-only map had the form
\begin{equation}
\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{Int}}
=
\sigma\!\left(c+\operatorname{logit}(\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{raw}})\right),
\label{eq:intercept-calibration}
\end{equation}
where $c$ was selected so that the validation mean matched the validation event rate. Isotonic regression estimated a non-decreasing mapping from raw validation probabilities to validation outcomes, with values outside the fitted range clipped to the fitted boundaries. Neither sensitivity map used test outcomes during fitting.

\section{Evaluation Measures}

The primary probability measures were the Brier score and log loss. For an evaluation sample $\mathcal{S}$,
\begin{align}
BS&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{S}}(\widehat\pi_{i,t}-Y_{i,t}^{(12)})^2,\label{eq:brier-score}\\
LL&=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{S}}\left[Y_{i,t}^{(12)}\log\widehat\pi_{i,t}+(1-Y_{i,t}^{(12)})\log(1-\widehat\pi_{i,t})\right].\label{eq:log-loss}
\end{align}
Lower values indicate better probability forecasts. The Brier score was also decomposed over ten equal-count probability bins into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty \citep{brier1950,murphy1973vector,dimitriadis2020_reliability}.

For bin $b$, let $n_b$ be its size, $\bar\pi_b$ its mean prediction, $\bar y_b$ its observed event rate, and $\bar y$ the overall event rate. The grouped components were
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Reliability}&=\sum_{b=1}^{10}\frac{n_b}{n}(\bar\pi_b-\bar y_b)^2,\\
\mathrm{Resolution}&=\sum_{b=1}^{10}\frac{n_b}{n}(\bar y_b-\bar y)^2,\\
\mathrm{Uncertainty}&=\bar y(1-\bar y).
\end{align}
The grouped Brier diagnostic equalled reliability minus resolution plus uncertainty. Equal-count bins were constructed by ranking predictions before division into deciles. The grouped value was not expected to equal the ungrouped score exactly.

ROC-AUC and PR-AUC measured discrimination. PR-AUC was reported because the serious-delinquency outcome was rare \citep{davis_goadrich_2006,saito2015prroc}. Calibration curves compared mean predicted PD with observed event frequency across ten bins. Annual test-period Brier scores described temporal variation. The monthly portfolio diagnostic compared observed defaults, $D_t=\sum_iY_{i,t}^{(12)}$, with predicted defaults, $\widehat D_t=\sum_i\widehat\pi_{i,t}$, using mean absolute count error and mean absolute relative error.

Test observations were also divided into ten quantile bins of $q_t$. Each bin recorded its stress-probability range, event rate, mean prediction, and model metrics. These bins described gradients within the terminal period. They did not supply independent macroeconomic episodes because each observation in every bin had a hard-stress indicator equal to one.

No rolling-origin HMM re-estimation, block bootstrap, loan-clustered uncertainty interval, or alternative HMM specification was performed. The analysis therefore reports point estimates for one chronological split. It does not treat the number of loan-months as the number of independent macroeconomic observations.
