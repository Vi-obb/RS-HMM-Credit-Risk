\section{Background and Problem}

Loan-level probability of default (PD) is the conditional probability that a particular loan enters a defined default state within a specified horizon, given the information available at the prediction date. This study uses a loan-month prediction unit and a twelve-month serious-delinquency outcome. For a loan $i$ observed in month $t$, the outcome is
\begin{equation}
Y_{i,t}^{(12)}
=
\mathbb{I}\!\left\{\max_{h=1,\ldots,12}D_{i,t+h}\geq 90\right\},
\label{eq:intro-default-indicator}
\end{equation}
where $D_{i,t+h}$ is days past due and the loan has not already reached 90 days past due at month $t$. The corresponding PD is
\begin{equation}
\pi_{i,t}^{(12)}
=
\Pr\!\left(Y_{i,t}^{(12)}=1\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right),
\label{eq:intro-pd-target}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}_{t}$ contains information available by month $t$. The event is therefore the first transition to 90 or more days past due during months $t+1$ to $t+12$. This threshold is an operational measure of serious mortgage delinquency and is consistent with regulatory treatment of material arrears as evidence of default \citep{bcbs_qis3_default,fed_basel2_default}.

Loan-level PD models must estimate probability levels as well as rank loans by risk. Discrimination measures whether higher-risk loans tend to receive higher scores. Calibration measures agreement between predicted probabilities and observed event frequencies. Both properties matter because a well-ranked score can still overstate or understate the level of risk \citep{brier1950,gneiting_raftery_2007,van_calster_2019}.

Logistic regression is a standard loan-level credit-scoring method. It provides an explicit probability function and coefficients with a direct log-odds interpretation \citep{hand_henley_1997,thomas2000survey,costa2020logistic}. A borrower-only specification, however, assumes that the relationship between borrower characteristics and default remains stable through calendar time. This restriction can be material when common economic conditions change the consequences of leverage, payment burden, interest rates, or recent delinquency.

Observed macroeconomic variables can be added to the logistic equation. Such models connect loan-level risk to the credit cycle and are useful in stress analysis \citep{bellotti2009credit,bellotti2013stress}. A purely additive specification still assumes that macroeconomic variables shift default risk without changing borrower sensitivities. It also represents a persistent and multidimensional economic state as a linear combination of contemporaneous indicators. These restrictions may be inadequate when the same borrower characteristic carries different risk under benign and stressed conditions.

\section{Research Gap and Aim}

The literature contains established loan-level scorecards, macro-augmented credit models, and latent-state credit-risk models. Their intersection is narrower. Borrower-only models often impose constant coefficients. Additive macroeconomic models do not allow the effect of borrower information to vary with the economic state. Regime-switching applications frequently address rating migrations, default intensities, or portfolio-level quantities rather than an interpretable loan-month PD \citep{bangia_et_al_2002,yu2018modeling}. This leaves an empirical question about whether a filtered macroeconomic regime probability can improve a transparent loan-level model through selected borrower--regime interactions.

The study aimed to develop and evaluate a regime-aware model for twelve-month mortgage PD. A two-state Gaussian hidden Markov model (HMM) reduced a set of public macroeconomic indicators to a filtered probability of stress. That probability entered a regularised logistic model directly and through selected interactions with borrower, loan, and payment-history information.

\section{Objectives and Research Questions}

The completed study had three objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It estimated macroeconomic regime probabilities using a two-state HMM.
    \item It developed a regime-aware loan-level PD model that combined borrower information with the estimated regime probabilities.
    \item It evaluated the regime-aware model against borrower-only and macro-augmented logistic baselines.
\end{enumerate}

The corresponding research questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What macroeconomic stress probabilities did the two-state HMM assign over the study period?
    \item How did the inclusion of the stress probability and borrower--regime interactions alter the loan-level PD specification?
    \item How did the regime-aware model compare with borrower-only and macro-augmented logistic baselines on calibration and discrimination?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contribution and Scope}

The methodological contribution is an interpretable link between a two-state macroeconomic HMM and a regularised loan-level logistic model. The regime probability is a continuous input. Selected interactions allow specified borrower effects to vary with that probability. The empirical contribution is an application to Freddie Mac mortgage origination and monthly performance data matched to public macroeconomic series. The evaluative contribution is a chronological comparison based on the Brier score, log loss, calibration diagnostics, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and monthly default-count error.

These contributions are limited in scope. The study does not claim that regime switching is new in credit risk. It tests a specific interpretable specification on one mortgage dataset and one terminal test period. The HMM parameters and factor transformations were estimated over the full study window, although the reported state probabilities were generated by forward filtering. The test-period stress probability was also nearly saturated. These features restrict real-time and cross-regime interpretation.
