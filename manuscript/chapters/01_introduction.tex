\chapter{Introduction}

This thesis studies whether latent macro regimes can improve loan-level probability of default estimation in a way that is interpretable, calibration-focused, and robust to regime transitions.

\section{Problem Statement}

The core problem is that pooled loan-level PD models can understate risk when borrower behavior changes across macro regimes.

\section{Novelty Framing}

The intended contribution is not a generic application of regime switching to consumer credit. The contribution is a calibration-first, interpretable framework that compares hard-state, soft-probability, and interaction-based regime-aware logistic PD specifications under controlled simulation and, later, real data.
