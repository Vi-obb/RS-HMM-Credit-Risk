\chapter{Results}

This chapter reports the empirical Freddie Mac and FRED experiment. The active outputs are stored under \texttt{reports/tables/} and \texttt{reports/figures/}; archived synthetic outputs are not used as evidence.

\section{Sample Coverage}

The experiment uses Freddie Mac sample cohorts from 2015 through 2024 after applying the 12-month look-ahead requirement. The 2025 cohort is excluded from the model-ready sample because it does not have sufficient observed future performance for the 12-month serious-delinquency label. The resulting model-ready panel contains 15,461,471 loan-month observations and 167,056 forward 90+ DPD events, for an overall event rate of 1.08 percent. Cohort-level coverage is written to \texttt{reports/tables/data\_coverage.csv}.

\section{Macro Regime Layer}

The current empirical pass restricts the macro HMM layer to the mortgage study window plus a 12-month lag lookback. The resulting FRED window runs from January 2014 through September 2024, rather than the full FRED history. A two-state Gaussian HMM is fit to monthly CPI inflation, Federal Funds rate, and unemployment. The stress probability used downstream is the forward-filtered probability
\[
    \hat p_t^{\mathrm{Stress}}
    =
    \Pr(R_t=\mathrm{Stress}\mid M_1,\ldots,M_t),
\]
so the stress signal at month $t$ is based on macro observations observed through month $t$ rather than future macro observations. The exported macro file also contains dynamic stress variables: \texttt{p\_stress\_lag\_3}, \texttt{p\_stress\_lag\_6}, \texttt{p\_stress\_lag\_12}, \texttt{delta\_p\_stress}, and \texttt{stress\_duration}. The filtered stress path and figure are written to the active processed-data and figures directories.

\section{Model Comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:test-metrics} summarizes the held-out test-period results for the current model comparison. The core thesis comparison remains borrower-only, macro-augmented, and regime-aware logistic PD models. This pass also reports a diagnostic lagged-stress regime-aware variant to test whether dynamic stress features improve performance. The held-out period covers 4,106,275 loan-month observations with a 0.657 percent event rate.

The clean regime-aware logit has the lowest held-out Brier score, but the improvement relative to the borrower-only baseline is very small: 0.006499 compared with 0.006502. The borrower-only model still has the best log loss. The lagged regime-aware variant has the highest ROC-AUC, followed by the macro logit, but both models overpredict the held-out event rate and perform worse on Brier score and log loss. The empirical conclusion is therefore mixed: the cleaner HMM stress signal provides a modest calibration gain by Brier score, but it does not clearly dominate the borrower-only benchmark as a probability model.

\section{Calibration And Slices}

Calibration-bin and Brier-decomposition outputs are written to the active reports directory. Validation-based Platt calibration is applied after regularization is selected on the validation split. On the test set, the clean regime-aware model has the lowest grouped reliability term, while the borrower-only model remains competitive and has the best log loss.

The test period contains only hard-stress observations under the 0.50 stress-probability threshold, so the regime-slice table does not provide a meaningful normal-versus-stress contrast for the held-out period. The test-period Brier scores for the borrower-only model are 0.005851 in 2023 and 0.007093 in 2024. The clean regime-aware model is nearly tied in 2023 and slightly lower in 2024, while the macro and lagged-regime variants deteriorate because their predicted probabilities rise faster than observed event rates.

\section{Interpretation}

The current empirical evidence should be interpreted carefully. This pass is methodologically stronger than the first run because it restricts the HMM to the mortgage study window, uses forward-filtered stress probabilities, tests a cleaner regime-aware specification without raw macro covariates, tunes regularization on validation Brier score, and calibrates final probabilities on the validation split. The clean regime-aware model slightly improves held-out Brier score and calibration-bin reliability relative to the borrower-only benchmark, but the borrower-only model still has the best log loss. The lagged stress interaction variant improves ranking performance but worsens probability quality. The thesis should therefore frame the result as a modest and mixed calibration finding rather than as broad predictive dominance of the regime-aware model.
