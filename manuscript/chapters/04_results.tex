\section{Sample and Outcome}

The eligible panel contained 15,461,471 loan-months from 438,713 loans across the 2015--2024 cohorts. It contained 167,056 twelve-month transitions to 90 or more days past due, an event rate of 1.0805\%. The training sample contained 8,179,648 observations with an event rate of 1.5191\%. The validation sample contained 3,175,548 observations with an event rate of 0.4982\%. The test sample contained 4,106,275 observations and 26,983 events, an event rate of 0.6571\%. The variation in event rates across the chronological samples shows that the probability level changed materially over time.

Table~\ref{tab:cohort-coverage} reports the retained cohort coverage. Earlier cohorts contributed longer histories and more loan-months. The 2023 and 2024 cohorts contributed fewer observations because the model-ready data ended in September 2024 and each included loan-month required twelve subsequent observed months. Cohort event rates were not monotonic. They ranged from 0.4361\% for the 2020 cohort to 2.0893\% for the 2019 cohort.

\section{Estimated Macroeconomic Regimes}

The HMM used 129 monthly factor observations from January 2014 to September 2024. The fitted stress probability ranged from approximately $1.63\times10^{-102}$ to a value numerically equal to one over that window. Ninety-nine months were below the hard-state threshold of 0.5 and 30 months were above it. Figure~\ref{fig:hmm-stress-path} shows the filtered probability path.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{../reports/figures/hmm_stress_probability.png}
    \caption{Forward-filtered probability of the HMM stress state, January 2014--September 2024. Factor transformations and HMM parameters were estimated over the full displayed window.}
    \label{fig:hmm-stress-path}
\end{figure}

The probability was nearly saturated during the test period. It ranged from 0.999903 to a value numerically indistinguishable from one, and every test observation had $q_t\geq0.5$. The test data therefore did not contain a hard normal-regime comparison. Quantile bins separated only small numerical differences near one. The retained HMM answered the first research question by assigning a persistent stress state throughout the terminal test period, but it provided weak variation for estimating cross-regime test performance.

\section{Validation Results}

The borrower-only model performed best on the validation sample under the primary Platt calibration. Its Brier score was 0.004645 and its log loss was 0.026122. The regime-aware values were 0.004682 and 0.027021. The macro-augmented values were 0.004683 and 0.026850. Borrower-only also had the highest validation ROC-AUC, 0.798634, and PR-AUC, 0.114809. The corresponding regime-aware values were 0.767182 and 0.105560. The validation evidence did not show an advantage for the regime-aware specification.

The later test ranking was not used to choose them.

\section{Causal Rolling-Origin HMM Diagnostics}

The rolling analysis fitted five deterministic HMM starts at each origin and selected training likelihood without using validation or test loan outcomes. Selected seeds were 101, 71, and 71. At the first origin, seeds 23 and 71 produced invalid transition matrices because one state had no observed outgoing transition; these starts were excluded. The remaining three first-origin starts and all five starts at each later origin converged to valid fitted transition matrices. This result illustrates why deterministic multiple starts were retained even though the number of states and covariance structure were fixed.

Table~\ref{tab:rolling-stress-diagnostics} reports stress diagnostics for the three test windows. Causal fitting changed the hard-state composition relative to the terminal full-window feature path: the first rolling test contained one hard-stress month, the second contained twelve, and the third contained eight. It did not produce a well-spread continuous probability. All first- and second-origin test months and eleven of twelve third-origin months had $q_t\leq0.001$ or $q_t\geq0.999$.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

The first research question asked what stress probabilities the two-state HMM assigned. The terminal HMM assigned every April 2023--September 2024 month to hard stress and produced probabilities above 0.999902. Causal rolling fits supplied both hard states across the combined tests, but 35 of 36 test months remained at or beyond 0.001 and 0.999. The answer is therefore that the fitted HMM identified persistent, sharply separated periods, but its posterior was too saturated to support a stable continuous cross-regime gradient.

The second question asked how the stress probability and interactions changed the PD specification. Equation~\eqref{eq:regime-aware-model} added a common $q_t$ term and allowed eight selected borrower, leverage, interest-rate, and payment-history sensitivities to vary with $q_t$. The structure remained a regularised logistic model and excluded the raw macroeconomic vector. It provided an explicit and inspectable route by which fitted macroeconomic conditions could alter loan-level log odds. The rolling results show that structural interpretability did not ensure temporal predictive stability.

The third question asked how the regime-aware model compared with borrower-only and macro-augmented baselines. In the original terminal test, regime-aware was best on Brier, log loss, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC, although borrower-only was best on validation and monthly count error. Under causal rolling evaluation, regime-aware won the first origin, lost the next two, and was worse than borrower-only on pooled proper scores, discrimination, and monthly count calibration. The answer is mixed and calibration-first: regime-aware can add period-specific predictive value, but borrower-only was the more stable pooled forecasting model.

\section{Overall Interpretation}

The original terminal result remains valid for the predictions and dates on which it was calculated. It is not the final general comparison. The causal rolling pass removed full-window information from factor scaling and HMM parameter estimation, tested three successive windows, and added temporally blocked paired uncertainty. Under that stricter design, the regime-aware advantage did not recur.

The final empirical conclusion is therefore narrower than a model-win claim. The regime-aware logistic model is an interpretable, period-dependent diagnostic: it states how selected loan sensitivities are allowed to change with a fitted latent macroeconomic condition, and it improved forecasts in one rolling origin and the terminal split. Borrower-only logistic regression is stronger on pooled causal Brier score, log loss, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and monthly count calibration. The evidence does not support universal predictive superiority for regime awareness.
