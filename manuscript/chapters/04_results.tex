\chapter{Results}

This chapter reports the empirical Freddie Mac and FRED experiment. The active outputs are stored under \texttt{reports/tables/} and \texttt{reports/figures/}; archived synthetic outputs are not used as evidence.

\section{Sample Coverage}

The experiment uses Freddie Mac sample cohorts from 2015 through 2024 after applying the 12-month look-ahead requirement. The 2025 cohort is excluded from the model-ready sample because it does not have sufficient observed future performance for the 12-month serious-delinquency label. The resulting model-ready panel contains 15,461,471 loan-month observations and 167,056 forward 90+ DPD events, for an overall event rate of 1.08 percent. Cohort-level coverage is written to \texttt{reports/tables/data\_coverage.csv}.

\section{Macro Regime Layer}

The macro layer uses FRED CPI inflation, Federal Funds rate, and unemployment from July 1954 through May 2026. A two-state Gaussian HMM produces filtered monthly stress probabilities and hard stress indicators. The filtered stress path is written to \texttt{data/processed/macro\_with\_hmm.csv}, and the corresponding figure is \texttt{reports/figures/hmm\_stress\_probability.png}.

\section{Model Comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:test-metrics} summarizes the held-out test-period results for the three approved logistic models. The held-out period covers 4,106,275 loan-month observations with a 0.657 percent event rate.

The macro-augmented model has the highest test ROC-AUC, but the difference relative to the borrower-only model is small. The borrower-only model has the strongest calibration-oriented performance by Brier score and log loss. Both macro-augmented specifications overpredict the held-out event rate, which raises their Brier scores despite similar discrimination.

\section{Calibration And Slices}

Calibration-bin and Brier-decomposition outputs are written to \texttt{reports/tables/calibration\_bins.csv} and \texttt{reports/tables/brier\_decomposition.csv}. The held-out calibration figure is \texttt{reports/figures/model\_calibration\_test.png}. The baseline model is still overconfident in the highest risk bins, but it is less overpredicted than the macro and regime-aware specifications on the test period.

The test period contains no hard-stress months under the 0.50 stress-probability threshold, so \texttt{reports/tables/metrics\_by\_regime.csv} reports the test observations as normal-regime and overall slices. Time-slice metrics are written to \texttt{reports/tables/metrics\_by\_time\_bucket.csv}. The test-period Brier score for the borrower-only model is 0.00942 in 2023 and 0.01444 in 2024, while the macro and regime-aware models deteriorate more sharply in 2024 because their predicted probabilities rise faster than observed event rates.

\section{Interpretation}

The current empirical evidence does not support the claim that the regime-aware logistic specification improves calibrated loan-level PD estimates on the held-out period. The HMM stress signal is interpretable and adds macro context, but the downstream regime-aware model is less calibrated than the borrower-only benchmark in this run. The thesis contribution should therefore be framed as a calibration-first empirical test of a transparent regime-aware PD framework, with the main finding that the added macro-regime layer did not improve held-out probability quality under the current specification.
