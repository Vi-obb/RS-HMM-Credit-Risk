\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

\section{Empirical Coverage}

The expanded empirical run uses Freddie Mac sample cohorts from 2015 through 2024 after enforcing the twelve-month look-ahead requirement. Although 2025 source files are included in ingestion, that cohort cannot contribute model-ready observations because twelve months of subsequent servicing performance are not yet available. The final model-ready panel contains 15,461,471 loan-month observations and 167,056 forward transitions to 90+ days past due, an overall event rate of 1.08\%.

The chronological split produces 8,179,648 training observations, 3,175,548 validation observations, and 4,106,275 test observations. The corresponding event rates are 1.519\%, 0.498\%, and 0.657\%. The decline from training to validation and the subsequent rise in the test period demonstrate why a random split would be inappropriate: base risk and the borrower mix change materially through calendar time.

Coverage is not uniform across cohorts. Earlier cohorts contribute longer performance histories, while 2023 and 2024 contribute fewer months because of the common September 2024 model-ready endpoint. The analysis therefore treats loan-months, not origination cohorts, as the prediction units and reports calendar-time diagnostics in addition to pooled scores.

\section{Expanded Macroeconomic Regime Layer}

The macro ingestion contains 862 monthly rows from July 1954 through May 2026, but the active HMM estimation window is restricted to January 2014--September 2024. The transformed candidate set is reduced to four adverse-oriented factors: housing/collateral, rate/refinancing, labour/income, and financial conditions. The two-state Gaussian HMM is fitted to those factors, and the state with the larger sum of adverse-oriented means is labelled stress.

Figure~\ref{fig:hmm-stress-path} displays the resulting stress probability. The path is informative in the earlier study window but becomes nearly degenerate in the terminal period. Across the test months, $q_t$ ranges from approximately 0.999903 to a value numerically indistinguishable from 1. Every test observation is consequently classified as hard stress under the threshold $q_t\geq0.5$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{../reports/figures/hmm_stress_probability.png}
    \caption{Forward-filtered probability of the HMM stress state over the mortgage study window. The probabilities are conditional on factor transformations and HMM parameters estimated over the full displayed window.}
    \label{fig:hmm-stress-path}

This saturation has two consequences. First, a hard normal-versus-stress test comparison is impossible because the test set contains no hard-normal months. Second, even decile labels provide little economic separation: the deciles partition extremely small numerical differences close to one. The stress-bin results are retained as diagnostics but cannot establish that the model performs differently across substantively distinct test regimes.

\section{Validation Evidence and Model Selection}

Regularisation is selected from $\{3\times10^{-6},10^{-5},3\times10^{-5}\}$ using Brier score on deterministic 500,000-row training and validation subsamples. The borrower-only, macro, stress-only, regime-aware-with-raw-macro, and dynamic-stress specifications select $3\times10^{-5}$, while the clean regime-aware model selects $3\times10^{-6}$. The fitted probabilities are then recalibrated on the full chronological validation set.

Validation evidence does not favour the clean regime-aware model. Under primary Platt calibration, the borrower-only model records a validation Brier score of 0.004645 and log loss of 0.026122. The clean regime-aware model records 0.004682 and 0.027021. The borrower-only model also has the strongest validation ROC-AUC (0.798634) and PR-AUC (0.114809) among the reported ladder. Thus, the eventual test advantage of the regime-aware model was not selected by looking at the test set and is not a uniformly recurring advantage across time.

Isotonic calibration improves validation Brier scores for several models. For the borrower-only model, isotonic Brier is 0.004629, while the clean regime-aware isotonic Brier is 0.004647. These results support reporting calibration sensitivity but do not change the validation ranking.

The nested ladder clarifies what produces the improvement. Adding $q_t$ without interactions does not improve probability quality: its Brier score is 0.006089. Adding observed macro variables to the interaction model also performs worse than the clean regime-aware specification. The evidence therefore favours the selected stress-by-borrower interaction structure on this test period, not the claim that every use of a macro regime improves PD estimation.

The dynamic-stress robustness model performs poorly. Its mean predicted PD is 0.000687 against an event rate of 0.006571, indicating severe underprediction after validation calibration. Its ranking metrics also deteriorate substantially. Lagged or duration effects should therefore not replace the clean model in the main specification without redesign and rolling-origin testing.

\section{Calibration Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:test-calibration} displays the test reliability curves generated by the empirical pipeline. The curves show that validation-fitted calibration does not fully eliminate temporal drift. Mean predicted PD exceeds the test event rate for each core model: 0.010747 for borrower-only and 0.010802 for regime-aware against 0.006571 observed. The regime-aware advantage is therefore a relative improvement in distribution-sensitive probability scores, not exact mean calibration.

The grouped Brier decomposition provides additional context. The clean regime-aware model has reliability 0.0000293 and resolution 0.0001017. Borrower-only has reliability 0.0000549 and resolution 0.0001006. On the grouped diagnostic, the regime-aware improvement is therefore primarily a lower reliability error while resolution remains similar. The observed-macro model has even lower grouped reliability (0.0000247), but its ungrouped Brier and ranking metrics are worse. This difference illustrates why binned decomposition should not be used alone.

Table~\ref{tab:calibration-sensitivity} compares calibration methods for the two central models. Isotonic calibration slightly improves the clean regime-aware test Brier and log loss relative to Platt, but it does not improve borrower-only performance on the test set. Because calibration methods were fitted on validation and the primary method was fixed as Platt, isotonic is reported as sensitivity rather than promoted after observing the test result.

\section{Temporal and Portfolio-Level Diagnostics}

The clean regime-aware model has a lower Brier score in both reported test-year slices. In 2023, its Brier score is 0.005432 compared with 0.005445 for borrower-only. In 2024, the values are 0.006546 and 0.006609. This consistency across the two terminal years is encouraging, although the slices share the same fitted HMM and both are classified as hard stress.

Portfolio timing is less favourable. Table~\ref{tab:monthly-count-error} reports the mean absolute monthly predicted-default-count error over 18 test months. Borrower-only has the lowest error at 952.5 defaults per month, compared with 965.3 for the clean regime-aware model. The mean absolute relative errors are 60.97\% and 63.67\%. Both are large, and the errors increase toward the end of the test period as predicted PDs rise faster than observed event rates.

The portfolio result tempers the pooled test advantage. The clean regime-aware model improves average loan-level probability scores and ranking but does not improve the average monthly level of predicted defaults. This distinction is material for a model intended to support portfolio planning.

\section{Interpretation and Unresolved Robustness Requirements}

The current evidence supports a narrow claim: on the 2023--2024 terminal test period, the clean regime-aware interaction model modestly improves Brier score, log loss, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC relative to borrower-only. It does not support a broad claim of dominance. Borrower-only is stronger on validation Brier and monthly default-count error; the direct stress-only, raw-macro interaction, and dynamic-stress variants do not improve probability quality; and the test-period stress signal is nearly saturated.

Three robustness requirements remain before journal submission. First, the factor transforms, scaler, and HMM must be re-estimated at multiple historical origins so that the regime probability is genuinely available in real time. Second, uncertainty around paired score differences should be estimated using calendar-block or loan-aware resampling rather than treating 4.1 million correlated loan-months as independent. Third, the near-saturated HMM must be stress-tested against alternative estimation windows, covariance restrictions, state-orientation rules, and factor scaling. These requirements are part of the empirical result, not optional presentation refinements.
