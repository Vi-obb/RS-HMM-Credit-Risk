\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\singlespacing
This thesis evaluates whether an interpretable macroeconomic regime signal improves twelve-month loan-level mortgage probability-of-default (PD) forecasts. Freddie Mac sample origination and monthly performance records for the 2015--2024 cohorts were linked to public macroeconomic series from Federal Reserve Economic Data. The eligible sample contained 438,713 loans, 15,461,471 loan-months, and 167,056 transitions to at least 90 days past due. A two-state Gaussian hidden Markov model compressed four adverse-oriented macroeconomic factors into a filtered stress probability. That probability entered a regularised logistic model directly and through eight selected interactions with borrower, loan, and payment-history characteristics. Borrower-only and additive macroeconomic logistic models provided the principal comparators. Validation-based Platt scaling supplied the primary calibrated probabilities.

In the original terminal test period, the regime-aware model had a Brier score of 0.006015 and log loss of 0.032945, compared with 0.006055 and 0.033106 for borrower-only. The difference was small, borrower-only remained stronger on validation and monthly default-count calibration, and terminal stress probabilities were nearly saturated at one. A predefined causal rolling-origin analysis therefore re-estimated factor transformations, scaling, and HMM parameters only through each training cutoff and evaluated three non-overlapping twelve-month test windows. Across 6,650,858 pooled rolling-test loan-months, borrower-only achieved lower Brier score and log loss, higher ROC-AUC and PR-AUC, and lower monthly count error than regime-aware. Three-month moving-block intervals for paired Brier and log-loss differences included zero. Stress probabilities remained extreme under causal fitting.

The evidence does not establish universal predictive superiority for the regime-aware model. Its advantage was period-dependent: it improved one causal origin and the original terminal split, but the gain did not persist across origins. The model is therefore retained as an interpretable diagnostic of changing borrower sensitivities under fitted macroeconomic conditions, while borrower-only logistic regression is the more stable pooled forecasting model in this study.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} probability of default; hidden Markov model; mortgage credit risk; calibration; rolling-origin validation; regime awareness.
