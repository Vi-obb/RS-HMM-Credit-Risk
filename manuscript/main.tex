\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}
This study develops and evaluates an interpretable regime-aware model for loan-level mortgage probability of default (PD). Monthly Freddie Mac origination and servicing records are combined with public macroeconomic indicators from the Federal Reserve Economic Data database. A two-state Gaussian hidden Markov model compresses housing, refinancing, labour-income, and financial-conditions information into a filtered macroeconomic stress probability. That probability enters a regularised logistic PD model both directly and through interactions with borrower and payment-history characteristics. The empirical design uses a twelve-month transition to serious delinquency, chronological training, validation, and test samples, and validation-based probability calibration. On 4,106,275 held-out loan-month observations, the primary Platt-calibrated regime-aware model records a Brier score of 0.006015, log loss of 0.032945, ROC-AUC of 0.827256, and PR-AUC of 0.160097. The corresponding borrower-only model records 0.006055, 0.033106, 0.821979, and 0.148361. These test-period gains are modest and do not extend uniformly to validation or monthly portfolio-count calibration. Moreover, the inferred stress probability is nearly saturated during the test period. The present draft therefore treats the regime-aware result as incremental and conditional evidence, and identifies rolling-origin estimation and regime-signal robustness as necessary publication checks.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} probability of default; mortgage credit risk; hidden Markov model; regime switching; calibration; logistic regression; macroeconomic stress
